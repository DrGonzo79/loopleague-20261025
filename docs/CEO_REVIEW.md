# CEO review

## Challenge

- **Premise:** A fad plus sponsor growth does not equal a platform market. The report's 2.8 index, 2/10 pain, decayed spike, and crowded ownership stack are explicit negatives.
- **Wedge:** “League platform” is too broad. A fixed-scope managed season attached to a funded brief is the only credible wedge.
- **ICP:** Schools are not buyers. Brand managers are buyers, agencies are the channel, faculty and captains are operational users.
- **GTM:** Search is nearly irrelevant and the founder's warm-network assumption is the largest untested dependency.
- **Economics:** One $12,500 season yields about $7,000 before labor and overhead. That is a project, not a company. Margin and repeat use must be measured.
- **Moat:** Brackets and ranking code are commodities. Only distribution, trust, consent operations, and historical performance can compound.
- **Architecture:** Video and minors would create heavy compliance surface. The demo should store no media and use deterministic rules.
- **Observability:** The important funnel is sponsor intro → scoped call → conditional budget → schools recruited → consent cleared → weekly retention. Product clicks are secondary.
- **Failure modes:** no warm intros, slow school approval, incomplete consent, manipulated reach, safety incident, trend evaporation, incumbent bundle.

## Decisions made

1. Reframed LoopLeague as internal tooling for a managed activation, not SaaS for schools.
2. Made faculty verification and guardian consent hard ranking gates.
3. Replaced simulated AI with an explainable formula and explicit fictional fixtures.
4. Added a kill test tied to eight warm introductions and a $12,500 price.
5. Kept Python undeployed and excluded video upload, public voting, accounts, and payments.
6. Scored the opportunity 48/100 and made the docs recommend demand validation before further build.
